\section{Scope}
This note records a three-run solver comparison for an illustrative
zero-charge, closed-FLRW trajectory. The numerical results have now been
independently reproduced using the reconstructed equations and initial data
specified below. This is a numerical reproduction of the assumed model, not a
derivation of the model from the DFM--MKC action or an empirical validation.

\section{Reproduction configuration}
The assumed potential and initial matter contents are
\[
U(\phi)=\frac12\phi^2+\frac14\phi^4,\qquad
\phi(0)=1,\quad \dot\phi(0)=0,
\]
\[
\rho_m(0)=0.1,\qquad \rho_r(0)=0.1,\qquad H(0)=0.
\]
Thus the initial total density is $\rho_0=0.75+0.1+0.1=0.95$ in the
chosen units. For curvature $k=+1$ and $G=1$, the initial scale factor must
be set from the Friedmann constraint, not from a truncated decimal:
\[
a_0=\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi G\rho_0}}
=0.3544695181296627\ldots
\]

The first-order system used for the reproduction is
\[
\dot a=aH,\qquad \dot\phi=v,\qquad
\dot v=-3Hv-U'(\phi),
\]
\[
\dot\rho_m=-3H\rho_m,\qquad
\dot\rho_r=-4H\rho_r,
\]
\[
\rho=\frac12v^2+U(\phi)+\rho_m+\rho_r,\qquad
p=\frac12v^2-U(\phi)+\frac13\rho_r,
\]
\[
\dot H=-4\pi G(\rho+p)+\frac{1}{a^2},\qquad
H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{1}{a^2}.
\]
The Friedmann equation is the constraint used to initialize $a_0$; the
evolution is integrated in cosmic time. The solver configurations used were
DOP853 with $(\mathrm{rtol},\mathrm{atol})=(10^{-10},10^{-12})$,
DOP853 with $(10^{-12},10^{-14})$, and RK45 with $(10^{-10},10^{-12})$.
An event detects the maximum-size turnaround when $H$ crosses from positive
to negative and another detects the contracting branch crossing $a=0.1$.

integration. The exact constraint initialization is therefore part of the
reproducibility specification.

\section{Result}
\textbf{RESULT:} The maximum-size turnaround time is stable across the
reported solver settings. The late contracting phase is less stable, with
the time to $a=0.1$ spanning $0.011002$ across the supplied runs. This
comparison is numerical evidence about this assumed initial-value problem,
not proof that the equations are physically correct.

Adaptive integration tolerances control an estimated numerical error for the
integrator. They do not validate the physical equations, the potential, or the
model assumptions.

\section{Weakest missing result}
A controlled endpoint study is still needed. Monitor curvature invariants,
including the Kretschmann scalar
\[
K=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
\]
alongside $a(t)$ and $H(t)$. Repeat the contracting-phase integration with
appropriate independent formulations and endpoint-aware variables, and
demonstrate convergence over a sequence of smaller scale factors. A divergence
of $K$ as $a\to0$, if established under the stated equations, would support
curvature singularity classification; it would not alone prove every form of
geodesic incompleteness. If $K$ remains bounded, further invariants and
extension analysis may still be required.
