## 1. Abstract theorem

Use conformal time t in [0,T], fixed k>=0. Let the Hilbert space be an orthogonal direct sum of degree blocks H_l (finite-dimensional blocks suffice here), and P_j project onto degrees <=j. Let X'= (C(t)+kS)X+f(t). S is a bounded skew-adjoint nearest-neighbor streaming operator with norm <=1. C is block diagonal outside a fixed low block 0..r; it is dissipative in this norm, and locally integrable in operator norm on the compact interval. This allows arbitrarily large but finite integrable opacity. All projections P_j for j>=r commute with C. Assume X(0) and f(t) are supported at degrees <=p, p>=r. Let M=||X(0)||+integral_0^T ||f||, f integrable. Define X_N, N>=p, by the compressed generator P_N(C+kS)P_N, with the same initial data and forcing. This is zero-terminal closure, not the usual Bessel outgoing closure. Regard X_N as embedded by zero extension. For r<=q<=N, at any t in [0,T],

    ||(I-P_N)X(t)|| <= M (kt)^(N+1-p)/(N+1-p)!,
    ||P_q(X-X_N)(t)|| <= M (kt)^(2N+2-p-q)/(2N+2-p-q)!.

Also ||X-X_N|| <= M (kt)^(N+1-p)/(N+1-p)! by the residual estimate below. These conservative bounds may be replaced by their minimum with 2M (or M for the tail). k=0 gives zero error, with positive exponent. They apply to finite larger sections as well as the infinite hierarchy. For sources or initial data above p, split into the low data and the tail; add at most 2 times the initial tail norm plus integrated tail forcing norm to the low-error bound, by contraction and linearity.

Proof: bounded integrable generators give unique absolutely continuous solutions via the integral equation/Picard series, and their propagators are contractions by differentiation of squared norm and dissipativity. The same is true on every compressed section or upper tail. For j>=p, project the exact equation onto degrees >=j+1. Initial data and forcing vanish there; only the streaming link from degree j enters. Thus the upper-tail norm is bounded by k times the integral of the degree-j norm, itself bounded by the upper tail starting at j. Starting from ||X||<=M and iterating gives the first factorial bound. The same argument applies to propagation from a low block for the adjoint time-reversed finite generator: the adjoint of a dissipative operator is dissipative, and the skew streaming sign reverses. Consequently ||P_q U_N(t,s) P_{degree N}|| <= (k(t-s))^(N-q)/(N-q)! by adjoint duality; when N=q use contraction. The error in P_N X satisfies the homogeneous compressed generator plus the single omitted boundary link k P_N S (I-P_N)X, with zero initial error. By variation of constants its projection onto P_q is bounded by

    M integral_0^t k (k(t-s))^(N-q)/(N-q)! (ks)^(N+1-p)/(N+1-p)! ds
      = M (kt)^(2N+2-p-q)/(2N+2-p-q)!.

The equality uses the integer beta integral, or repeated elementary integration. This is the two-way propagation estimate. For a full-state residual, the zero-extended X_N solves the full generator with residual -k(I-P_N)S X_N. Hence ||X-X_N|| <= k integral_0^t ||(I-P_N)S X_N||. Applying the same tail bound within X_N to degree N gives the claimed full-state bound. This residual is also an a posteriori criterion: a validated integral of the computed boundary norm, plus certified time-integration error, bounds the full error without an infinite reference. A floating point quadrature alone is not a validated integral. QED.

Conditional feedback version: suppose a further real-linear operator B(t) is supported wholly in degrees <=r (possibly including additional finite-dimensional matter variables in that block), with symmetric part <=mu(t) I for nonnegative integrable mu. Then rescale the solution by exp(-integral_0^t mu). The new local operator C+B-mu I is dissipative, and the theorem applies. In original variables multiply the right sides by exp(integral_0^T mu). This is a theorem for the stated operator structure, not a verification of a specific metric constraint elimination. In particular it does not yet supply a bound independent of scalar mass, opacity, and k for the actual self-consistent system.

## 2. Streaming norm and actual collision identities

For scalar Legendre streaming X_l' = k[l X_(l-1)-(l+1)X_(l+1)]/(2l+1), set y_l=sqrt(2l+1) X_l. Adjacent skew coefficients are c_l=(l+1)/sqrt((2l+1)(2l+3)). Multiplication by mu on L2([-1,1], dmu/2) in the orthonormal Legendre basis is symmetric tridiagonal with c_l, norm <=1 because |mu|<=1. Diagonal phase conjugation followed by multiplication by i transforms it to the real skew streaming operator, so its norm is <=1. Restrictions to l>=2, finite sections, direct sums, and addition of a streaming-zero baryon velocity have the same upper bound. Collisionless massless-neutrino brightness has precisely this recurrence, including the low rows under prescribed Newtonian potentials (with forcing confined to low degrees).

Physical scalar photon brightness: temperature multipoles Theta_l=F_gamma_l/4, linear polarization brightness P_l=G_gamma_l/4 in the Ma–Bertschinger Legendre convention (P_l is NOT the spin-2 E_l). Let R=3rho_b/(4rho_gamma)>0, R'=Hc R, Hc>=0, Gamma>=0. Let v_b=theta_b/k, v_gamma=3Theta_1. With metric potentials prescribed, the forcing terms are Phi' in Theta_0, k Psi/3 in Theta_1, and k Psi in v_b'. The homogeneous Thomson dipole terms are Theta_1'=Gamma(v_b/3-Theta_1), v_b'=-Hc v_b+Gamma(v_gamma-v_b)/R. All intensity multipoles l>=2 have -Gamma Theta_l plus the quadrupole source Gamma Pi/10 at l=2. All polarization brightness multipoles have -Gamma P_l plus Gamma Pi/2 at l=0 and Gamma Pi/10 at l=2, with Pi=Theta_2+P_0+P_2. Intensity monopole has no collision term. Both brightness chains stream by the Legendre recurrence. The physical energy is

    ||X||^2 = sum_l (2l+1)(Theta_l^2+P_l^2) + R v_b^2/3.

The time-dependent change of coordinates b=sqrt(R/3) v_b produces a fixed Hilbert norm; the baryon Hubble drag then contributes -Hc b/2 to b'. The dipole/baryon energy derivative from drag and Hubble is exactly -(2Gamma/3)(v_gamma-v_b)^2-Hc R v_b^2/3. On the collision source block (Theta_2,P_0,P_2), let W=diag(5,1,5). The collision matrix divided by Gamma is L=-I+(1/10,1/2,1/10)^T(1,1,1). W L=L^T W, eigenvalues -1,-1,-3/10. Equivalently its quadratic form is -5 Theta_2^2-P_0^2-5 P_2^2+Pi^2/2 <= -3/10(5 Theta_2^2+P_0^2+5 P_2^2) by weighted Cauchy-Schwarz. All remaining diagonal collision rows dissipate energy. The low block must include degrees 0..2, so r=2. These calculations justify the theorem for this standard prescribed-metric physical photon brightness system, including coupled baryon velocity (not baryon density feedback). No derivative of Gamma is used.

Repository-specific E row: the inspected file instead uses an E chain starting at l=2 with ordinary Legendre streaming and Pi=Theta_2+6 E_2. Its given collision block on (Theta_2,E_2) is Lr=-I+(1/10,1/10)^T(1,6), symmetric in W_r=diag(5,30), with eigenvalues -1,-3/10. The energy with weights (2l+1) for Theta and 6(2l+1) for E and E_1=0 is dissipative for these rows. The theorem therefore covers the mathematical recurrence in that module with the prescribed low-row completion used in verify.py. This does NOT establish that its E streaming coefficients or conversion are equivalent to the physical spin-2 E hierarchy. That is a separate source-level normalization/derivation obligation. Do not use this stability proof as a polarization equivalence certificate.

## 3. Quadrupole and metric-slip error; nonuniform fixed-cutoff obstruction

For p=q=2 the exponent is D=2N-2. In the physical brightness convention sigma_gamma=F_gamma2/2=2Theta_2, so |sigma_gamma-sigma_gamma,N| <= (2/sqrt(5)) M (kt)^D/D!. For neutrino brightness F_nu with weighted norm sum(2l+1)|F_nu,l|^2, sigma_nu=F_nu2/2, giving coefficient 1/(2sqrt(5)). With prescribed a, rho, pressures and same k>0, the traceless Einstein relation k^2(Phi-Psi)=12 pi G a^2 sum_j (rho_j+p_j)sigma_j transmits these bounds linearly to the *reconstructed metric slip*. This comparison alone does not establish convergence of the self-consistent potentials or scalar perturbations. The actual full RHS can involve a derivative of anisotropic stress and thus the octupole, as well as opacity-dependent terms. A proof of uniform joint Einstein–Boltzmann averaging needs the low-block feedback estimate after a regular transformation and must include the required extra degrees in r. This remains OPEN.

A fixed cutoff is not justified uniformly as kT tends to infinity. In collisionless streaming with initial monopole one and all other degrees zero, the infinite solution is X_l=j_l(kt), with norm one by unitary streaming. Each fixed j_l(x) tends to zero as x tends to infinity, so the norm outside any fixed N tends to one. In fact the finite-section monopole cannot approximate the infinite monopole uniformly for large kt: it is a finite sum sum_j w_j exp(i omega_j kt), where w_j>=0, sum w_j=1 are the spectral weights of the monopole for the finite skew generator. Group distinct real frequencies. Its long-time mean square equals sum_j w_j^2 >=1/(N+1), whereas j_0(x)=sin(x)/x tends to zero. Thus there is a nonzero limsup error at fixed N. This is already a counterexample with no collisions and no metric forcing. For a sufficient large-k choice at fixed T, use Stirling's lower bound D! >= (D/e)^D: choose D>e kT with a fixed multiplicative margin, and the factorial estimate decays exponentially in D. In Jeans scaling k=sqrt(m kappa), a cutoff growing at least as a sufficiently large constant times sqrt(m) is sufficient for this estimate on fixed conformal intervals; this is not a necessary optimal asymptotic cutoff rate.
