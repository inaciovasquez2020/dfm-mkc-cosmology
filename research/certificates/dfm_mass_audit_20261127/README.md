## PROVED: uniform quadratic background convergence

Assume the homogeneous canonical quadratic complex field obeys psi''+3H psi'+m² psi=0, a>0, H>=0 nonincreasing, and m>0. Define rho=(|psi'|²+m²|psi|²)/2, E=a³rho, J=a³ Re(conj(psi)psi'), Q=a³ Im(conj(psi)psi'). Then E²>=m²(J²+Q²), Q is conserved, and I=E+(3/2)HJ obeys I'=(3/2)H'J. At the circular initial point i, E_i=m|Q|>0 and J_i=0. Thus E>=E_i on the entire compact interval, including times before i.

Let e=max 3H/(2m), M=max E/E_i. Since |mJ|/E_i<=sqrt(M²-1), the integrated identity yields |E(t)/E_i-1| <= (epsilon(t)+|epsilon(t)-epsilon(i)|)sqrt(M²-1)<=2e sqrt(M²-1), using monotonicity of H. Evaluate at the maximum. If M>1 and 2e<1, divide the squared inequality by M-1 to get M<=(1+4e²)/(1-4e²)=K. If M=1 the bound is trivial. Hence 1<=E/E_i<=K.

For a fixed finite redshift interval [0,Z] containing the circular initial point, match the present density E(0)=rho_c0>0 with a(today)=1. The continuity equation and |p|<=rho imply rho(z)<=rho_c0(1+z)^6. With fixed nonnegative baryon, radiation and cosmological constant contributions, the Friedmann equation bounds H uniformly in m on this interval. Hence e=O(m^-1) and K-1=O(m^-2). The ratio E/E(0) belongs to [1/K,K]. With the matched dust model H_d²=H0²[Omega_L+Omega_b(1+z)^3+Omega_c(1+z)^3+Omega_r(1+z)^4], the ratio H²/H_d² is a convex combination of 1 and E/E(0). Thus H/H_d and H_d/H are in [1/sqrt(K),sqrt(K)]. For positive redshifts, both radial and comoving transverse distances have the same ratio bounds. The volume distance, a weighted geometric mean, inherits them. At fixed common distance amplitude this proves O(m^-2) convergence of the BAO prediction vector. The profiled Gaussian score y^T W y -(f^T W y)^2/(f^T W f), W=C^-1, is smooth at the nonzero dust vector f. Thus its difference is O(m^-2) as well. This is an upper bound, not a monotonicity theorem, a lower bound on model discrepancy or a proof that every finite mass fits worse. The theorem applies along any sequence of calibrated solutions with masses tending to infinity; existence of such an unbounded sequence is not proved. A separate present-circular branch does have initial data for every positive mass; this relaxes the original historical circular initial condition.
